How to Meal Prep for a 9 to 6 Job in Tokyo

Master your Sunday kitchen routine to keep your work week lunches nutritious and stress free in Tokyo.
August 13, 2026 · 3 min · KinnikuGlow

You are likely exhausted by Friday evening and dread the thought of packing a lunch for Monday morning. Mastering your Sunday meal prep will save you time and keep your energy levels steady throughout your long work days.

Batch Cooking Basics#

Dedicate two hours on Sunday afternoon to cook large portions of simple staples that work well for several days. Choose one protein, one carbohydrate, and two vegetable options to mix and match. This prevents your lunches from feeling like a chore while ensuring you have healthy food ready when your alarm goes off.

Focus on proteins that stay moist after reheating, such as baked chicken thighs or firm tofu seasoned with shoyu and ginger. Boiling a batch of eggs is another simple way to add protein to your bento box in seconds. Keep your seasoning minimal during prep so you can add fresh sauces or dressings right before you leave for the office.

Smart Vegetable Prep#

Vegetables in Tokyo are often sold in small bundles that wilt if left in the crisper drawer for too long. Wash and chop your greens like spinach or komatsuna immediately after returning from the supermarket. Blanching these leafy greens for thirty seconds before storing them helps them retain their color and texture for your lunch.

Root vegetables such as carrots and daikon hold up perfectly in the fridge for the entire week. Roast a large tray of them with olive oil and salt to provide a satisfying crunch to your meals. When you have these basics ready, you can assemble your containers in less than five minutes each night.

Carbs and Storage#

Choose grains that do not dry out in the microwave, such as brown rice or quinoa. Cook a large batch in your rice cooker and portion it into individual containers while the rice is still warm to keep it fluffy. Avoid complex recipes that require multiple pots and pans because you want a routine that you can actually maintain.

Invest in high quality glass containers that seal tightly to prevent any leaks in your bag during your morning commute on the train. Place your prepared meals in the coldest part of your refrigerator to ensure they stay fresh until Thursday. If you need to prepare food for Friday, consider keeping those items in the freezer until Thursday night.

Your Weekly Next Step#

Choose three recipes you enjoy and create a simple shopping list before you head to your neighborhood supermarket this weekend. Start small by prepping just three days of lunches rather than the full week to build your confidence in the kitchen.
