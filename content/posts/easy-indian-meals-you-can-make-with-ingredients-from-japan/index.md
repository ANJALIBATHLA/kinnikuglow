Finding the right spices and ingredients for Indian cooking does not have to be a struggle when you live in Tokyo. You can create flavorful meals by utilizing the fresh vegetables and pantry staples available at your neighborhood supermarket.

## Use local produce for base curries

Japanese supermarkets are excellent for sourcing high quality ginger, garlic, and onions. These three ingredients form the base of almost every delicious curry. You can find large bags of onions at stores like Life or Summit, which are perfect for caramelizing to build depth in your dishes. Minced ginger and garlic are available in tubes if you are short on time, though fresh versions offer a brighter flavor.

## Master the tomato base

Many Indian dishes rely on a tomato base that is easy to replicate in Japan. Look for canned tomatoes in the pasta aisle of your local supermarket. If you cannot find crushed tomatoes, canned diced tomatoes work just as well when simmered until they break down. You can add a splash of plain yogurt to thicken the sauce and add a subtle tang that balances the spices.

## Find spices at import shops

While your local supermarket provides the fresh ingredients, you might need to visit a specialty shop for specific spices. Kaldi Coffee Farm is a reliable place to find curry powder, turmeric, and cumin seeds. If you live near Shin Okubo or Ueno, you can find dedicated Indian spice shops that sell lentils and basmati rice in bulk. Having a small stash of these essentials makes it easy to throw a meal together on a weeknight after work.

## Simple substitutions for authentic flavor

You do not need to hunt for every single traditional ingredient to make a satisfying meal. If a recipe calls for paneer, you can use firm tofu pressed under a heavy weight for a similar texture. Coconut milk is readily available in most larger supermarkets in the international food section. If you need cream, the heavy cream found in the dairy aisle is a perfect substitute for cooking.

## Next steps for your kitchen

Start by picking one simple dish like a vegetable dhal or a basic chicken curry. Buy your onions, ginger, and garlic this week and dedicate one hour on Sunday to prepping your aromatics. Having the base of your meal ready in the fridge turns a daunting recipe into a quick ten minute dinner.
