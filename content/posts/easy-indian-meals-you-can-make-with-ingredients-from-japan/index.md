You are staring at your kitchen shelves after a long day at the office and wondering how to make a flavorful dinner without trekking to a specialty import store. Making Indian food in Tokyo is entirely possible when you learn which staples are already hiding in your local supermarket.

## Finding the right base

Most Japanese grocery stores carry a surprising amount of pantry items you can use for Indian cooking. Look for canned tomatoes, which are essential for rich curry bases, often found near the pasta sauce section. You can find high quality ginger and garlic in the produce aisle, either fresh or conveniently pre minced in tubes if you are short on time.

[IMAGE: A selection of fresh ginger garlic and canned tomatoes on a wooden counter]

When it comes to protein, chicken breast or thigh works perfectly for classic butter chicken or spicy curries. If you want a vegetarian option, firm tofu from your neighborhood store works well as a substitute for paneer. Press the tofu for twenty minutes to remove excess moisture before pan frying it to give it a firmer texture.

## Essential spice substitutions

You might not find a wide range of individual Indian spices in every corner shop, but you can build a solid foundation with basics. Turmeric, cumin, and chili powder are becoming more common in larger supermarkets like Seiyu or Life. Look for the spice aisle labels that indicate standard seasonings.

If you cannot find a specific spice, curry powder is your best friend for a quick flavor boost. Many Japanese brands sell mild curry powders that serve as an excellent base for a homemade sauce. You can deepen the flavor by adding a splash of yogurt, which is readily available in the dairy section, to provide that signature creamy texture.

## Quick meal prep tips

Cook a large batch of rice to keep in your fridge throughout the week. Japanese short grain rice works well with Indian curries, though it has a different texture than basmati. If you want to mimic the fluffiness of restaurant style rice, rinse it thoroughly until the water runs clear before cooking.

[IMAGE: A container of prepped rice and a small bowl of homemade curry]

Portion your curry into small containers for your work lunches. These dishes often taste better the next day after the flavors have had time to meld in the refrigerator. Just remember to pack your rice separately so it does not get soggy before you heat it up at your desk.

## Your next kitchen step

Take a walk through your local supermarket this week specifically to look for canned tomatoes and ginger tubes. Once you have those two basics, try making a simple tomato based sauce with chicken or chickpeas. Build your spice collection slowly as you find what you enjoy cooking most.
