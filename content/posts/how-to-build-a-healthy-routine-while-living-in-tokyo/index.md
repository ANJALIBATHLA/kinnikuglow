You feel the pressure of the Tokyo commute and long office hours, but you can still find time to prioritize your health. Building a sustainable routine is not about major life changes but about small, consistent choices that fit into your day.

## Simplify your nutrition

[IMAGE: A bento box filled with fresh vegetables, grilled fish, and brown rice]

Focus on what you can easily find at your local supermarket or convenience store. Pick up items like pre washed salad greens, packs of natto, or grilled saba mackerel from the refrigerated section at Life or Seijo Ishii. You do not need to cook elaborate meals to eat well. Keep your kitchen stocked with eggs and frozen vegetables so you always have a base for a quick dinner when you return home late.

## Find movement that fits

You do not need an expensive gym membership to stay active in the city. Incorporate movement into your commute by getting off one station early and walking the rest of the way. If you prefer structured exercise, search for local community gyms known as ward sports centers. They often allow you to pay per session, which keeps things flexible if your work schedule changes. 

## Optimize your sleep

Tokyo life often involves bright lights and noise that can disrupt your rest. Create a wind down ritual that starts thirty minutes before you want to fall asleep. Put your phone away and dim the lights in your apartment to help your body transition from the busyness of the city to rest. A consistent wake up time, even on weekends, helps regulate your internal clock and makes those early Monday morning trains feel slightly more manageable.

## Keep your routine flexible

Life in Tokyo is unpredictable, and your routine should be able to adapt. If you miss a workout or end up eating out with colleagues, do not feel like you have failed. Pick up your healthy habits again at the next meal or the next day. The goal is to build a lifestyle that supports you over the long term rather than following a strict set of rules.

Start by choosing one change to implement this week, such as adding a vegetable to your lunch or taking a longer walk home. Consistency matters more than perfection.
