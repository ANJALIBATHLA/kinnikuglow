You probably find it difficult to stay energized through long Tokyo office hours when your lunch is just a convenience store rice ball. 

Packing your own protein rich lunch saves you money and keeps your nutrition on track during busy work weeks. You can easily build balanced meals using ingredients from your local supermarket like Life, Seiyu, or Maruetsu.

## Keep it simple with chicken and tofu

Focus on lean proteins that are easy to prepare in advance and taste great cold or at room temperature. Salada chicken, which you can find in the refrigerated section of most convenience stores or supermarkets, is a ready to eat staple. You can slice it over a bed of spinach or mixed greens. 

Firm tofu, or momen dofu, is another excellent plant based protein. Press the water out of the block, cube it, and toss it with a sesame dressing and steamed edamame. Edamame, found in the frozen section, is a quick way to add extra protein and fiber to any container.

## Use small containers for easy transport

You need leakproof containers that fit easily into your bag for the crowded train ride to work. Silicone cups are useful for separating wet ingredients like sauces or pickled vegetables from your main protein source. 

If you prefer hot lunches, look for thermal food jars that hold their temperature for several hours. These work well for soups or stews made with lean ground meat and root vegetables. Making a larger batch of curry or vegetable stew on Sunday allows you to scoop a portion into your jar every morning before you head to the station.

## Add flavor with Japanese pantry staples

Plain protein can feel boring after a few days, so use Japanese seasonings to change the flavor profile. Shoyu, miso, and toasted sesame oil transform simple chicken or fish into something more satisfying. 

Keep a small jar of shio koji in your fridge to marinate your protein the night before. This natural ingredient tenderizes meat and adds a deep savory flavor without much effort. Sprinkle some furikake over your grains or protein to add texture and a punch of saltiness before you seal your lunch box.

[IMAGE: A variety of Japanese seasonings and grains on a kitchen counter]

## Plan your shopping trips

Check your local supermarket inventory to see what is consistently available near your home or office. Buy your proteins and vegetables in bulk during your weekend shop to streamline your prep. 

Choose one or two protein sources for the week and cycle through different vegetables and dressings. Prepare your base ingredients on Sunday night, then assemble your containers each evening to save time during your busy Tokyo mornings.
