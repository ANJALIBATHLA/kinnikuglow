You are exhausted from your commute and the last thing you want to do is spend your Sunday night standing in front of a hot stove. 

Cooking for one or two in a small Tokyo kitchen does not have to turn into a marathon session. Focus on small, manageable tasks that get you through the work week without the stress.

## Master the supermarket shortcuts

Stop trying to chop every single vegetable from scratch. Your local supermarket like Life, Seiyu, or Aeon offers pre cut vegetable packs that save you significant time. 

[IMAGE: A selection of pre cut stir fry vegetable bags at a Tokyo supermarket]

Pick up bags of mixed greens or stir fry medleys. These go directly from the bag into a frying pan or a steaming bowl. You can also buy frozen broccoli, edamame, and corn. These are already washed and portioned so you can grab exactly what you need without worrying about spoilage.

## Build your protein rotation

Protein takes the longest to cook, so keep your methods basic to avoid cleaning multiple pots. Buy a pack of lean chicken breast or salmon fillets from the grocery store. Season them simply with salt, pepper, or a splash of soy sauce.

Bake a tray of protein in the oven while you do something else. Once it cools, portion it into containers. Having pre cooked protein ready means you can toss it into a bowl of salad, put it over rice, or eat it with your pre cut vegetables. 

## Assemble instead of cook

You do not need to follow complicated recipes to have a healthy lunch. Think of your meals as assemblies of pre made components. 

Start with a base of microwaveable rice, such as Sato no Gohan {{< affiliate "sato-no-gohan" >}}, which is ready in two minutes. Add your pre cooked protein and a handful of greens. Drizzle a high quality sesame dressing or soy sauce over the top. This approach minimizes your time in the kitchen while ensuring you have a filling meal for the office.

## Keep your flavor profile simple

Variety in your food is nice, but it requires too many specialty ingredients. Pick two or three sauces you enjoy and stick to them for the week. 

Keep a bottle of ponzu or mentsuyu in your fridge. These are staples in Japan for a reason. They work on almost any protein or vegetable combination. When you keep your seasonings consistent, you spend less time shopping and less money on pantry items that might expire.

## The next step

Choose one protein and two vegetable options for your next grocery run. Prep them in one hour or less this weekend and see how much easier your lunch hour feels on Tuesday.
