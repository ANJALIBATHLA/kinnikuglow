You are staring at your fridge after a late night shift at the office and realizing you have absolutely nothing healthy to eat. Making nutritious food in Tokyo does not have to mean spending your entire Sunday chopping vegetables in a tiny kitchen. 

## Use Convenience Store Staples

Convenience stores like Lawson or FamilyMart offer more than just fried chicken. Pick up pre washed bags of salad greens, packs of boiled eggs, and blocks of tofu to skip the prep work entirely. These items serve as the base for almost any meal. You can assemble a nutrient dense bowl in less than three minutes by combining these with a shelf stable grain like microwaveable brown rice.

## Master the Supermarket Shortcut

Grocery stores like Life or Seiyu stock pre cut vegetable mixes meant for stir fries or miso soup. Use these mixes to bypass the most time consuming part of cooking. Toss a bag into a frying pan with some pre cooked chicken breast or frozen seafood mix to finish your dinner quickly. This method keeps your kitchen cleanup minimal since you only have one pan to wash.

## Rely on Frozen Ingredients

Freezing is your best tool for managing a hectic Tokyo lifestyle. Buy bags of frozen broccoli, spinach, or edamame from your local supermarket and keep them in your freezer at all times. You can steam these in the microwave to add fiber and color to any meal without needing extra pots or pans. Frozen fish fillets are another great option because they often come individually wrapped and ready to cook straight from the freezer.

## Focus on Batch Cooking Basics

Batch cooking does not require you to prepare seven days of complicated meals. Choose one protein source like ground pork or salmon and cook a large portion at once. Divide it into small containers to use over two or three days. You can alternate the flavors by changing the sauce or the side dish each night. This creates variety without doubling your time in the kitchen.

## Start Small

Pick one category to focus on for your first week of meal prep. Start by prepping your proteins in advance or by ensuring you always have pre washed greens in the fridge. Success in the kitchen is about making small changes that you can actually maintain through a busy work schedule. Focus on consistent, easy habits instead of striving for perfection.
