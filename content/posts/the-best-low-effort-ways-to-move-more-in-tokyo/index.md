You are likely already walking more than you realize just by navigating your commute. Adding small, intentional movements to your existing Tokyo routine builds strength without requiring a gym membership.

## Master your commute

The most effective way to move more is to stop treating your commute as dead time. Skip the elevator and take the stairs at stations like Shinjuku or Tokyo Station. Most platforms have clear signage for stairs, which are often located away from the primary escalators. 

[IMAGE: A person walking up a clean, bright set of stairs in a Tokyo subway station]

You can also get off one stop early on your bus or train line. Walking that extra ten minutes through a quiet residential neighborhood or a busy shopping street provides a consistent cardio boost. If you work in an office building, try to take a walk during your lunch break rather than staying at your desk. Many office districts have small parks or green spaces nearby that offer a quick escape from the fluorescent lights.

## Make errands active

Shopping for groceries in Tokyo is a great opportunity to increase your daily step count. Instead of doing one massive haul at a large supermarket, stop at local shops on your way home. Carrying two smaller bags of groceries from a store like Life or Summit forces you to engage your core and muscles. 

Keep a reusable bag in your purse so you are always prepared to walk to the store. If you need to visit a pharmacy or a convenience store, walk there instead of taking a taxi or a bus. The distance between blocks in Tokyo is often shorter than it looks on a map.

## Plan active weekends

Use your weekends to explore areas of the city that require walking rather than sitting in a cafe. Neighborhoods like Yanaka or Shimokitazawa are designed for strolling through narrow streets and alleys. Set a goal to find a new shrine or park each Saturday morning.

[IMAGE: A view of a quiet, tree lined street in a Tokyo residential neighborhood]

Parks like Yoyogi or Shinjuku Gyoen are perfect for long walks if you want to avoid traffic. If you prefer structured movement, look for public sports centers managed by the ward office. These facilities often offer pay as you go access to walking tracks or swimming pools for a small fee.

## Build the habit

Start by choosing one change to make this week. Pick a specific station stairwell or a new walking route from your office. Consistency matters more than intensity when you are trying to stay active in a busy city. Once you become comfortable with one small change, add another to your routine.
