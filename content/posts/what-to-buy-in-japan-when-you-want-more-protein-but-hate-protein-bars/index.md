You are staring at the snack aisle in a Lawson or 7-Eleven, feeling tired of chalky protein bars that taste like cardboard. Finding high protein fuel in Tokyo does not have to mean relying on processed supplements.

## Look for chicken at the counter

The hot food counter is your best friend when you need a quick protein boost between meetings. Almost every convenience store in the city offers grilled chicken skewers known as yakitori. Look for momo, which is thigh meat, or sasami, which is chicken tenderloin. These options are easy to eat on the go and provide a substantial amount of protein without any added sugar or artificial sweeteners.

## Choose boiled eggs for a simple fix

Boiled eggs are a staple in every Japanese supermarket and convenience store. You can find them in packs of two near the refrigerated deli section. They are pre-peeled and usually lightly salted, making them the most convenient whole food snack available. Keep a pack in your office fridge for those afternoons when your lunch leaves you feeling hungry before you even leave your desk.

## Stock up on canned fish

Canned mackerel or iwashi is a protein powerhouse that stays fresh in your pantry for months. You can find these tins in the back aisles of any local Life or Summit supermarket. Look for labels that say mizuni, which means the fish is packed in water or brine rather than oil or heavy sauces. Simply drain the tin and add the fish to a bowl of rice or serve it alongside a salad for a balanced meal.

## Pick edamame from the frozen section

Frozen edamame is one of the most underrated protein sources you can find in Japan. You can buy large bags in the frozen section of any supermarket and keep them in your freezer at home. Pour a handful into a bowl and let them thaw for ten minutes or run them under warm water to heat them up. They contain fiber and plant based protein, making them an excellent choice if you prefer a light snack after a gym session.

## Try plain Greek yogurt

Greek yogurt is becoming much easier to find in Japanese grocery stores. Look for the Oikos brand {{< affiliate "oikos-yogurt" >}} in the refrigerated dairy aisle. It is thick, creamy, and contains significantly more protein than standard yogurt varieties. Check the label for plain or sugar free versions to avoid unnecessary additives. Pair it with a few almonds or a piece of fruit if you need a bit more energy.

## Keep your protein intake consistent

Focusing on these whole food options keeps your diet varied and interesting. Start by picking up one extra pack of eggs or a tin of fish during your next grocery run. Integrating these simple items into your daily routine helps you meet your fitness goals without forcing yourself to eat snacks you do not enjoy.
