You feel drained after a long day at the office and the thought of heading to a crowded gym seems impossible. Finding time for movement while managing a 9 to 6 schedule in Tokyo requires a shift in how you view exercise.

[IMAGE: A pair of sneakers placed neatly by a front door in a small Tokyo apartment]

## Utilize your commute

Walking to and from the train station is the most reliable way to increase your daily step count. Try getting off one stop early to add an extra ten minutes of walking to your morning or evening routine. 

If you take the Tokyo Metro, look for the stairs instead of the escalator whenever possible. These short bursts of activity add up significantly over the course of a work week.

## Move during your lunch hour

Step away from your desk when your lunch break begins. Even if you only have twenty minutes, a brisk walk around the neighborhood refreshes your mind and keeps your body moving.

Many local parks or quiet side streets in districts like Ebisu or Kanda offer space to clear your head. If you prefer to stay indoors, look for stretching routines that you can perform in a small space. 

## Keep evening workouts short

Avoid the pressure of hour long gym sessions after a taxing day at work. A fifteen minute session at home is much more sustainable than skipping a longer workout because you feel tired.

Focus on bodyweight exercises like squats or lunges that require no equipment. Consistency matters more than intensity when you are balancing a full time job.

[IMAGE: A yoga mat laid out on a clean floor near a window]

## Prepare your gear early

Pack your gym bag the night before or lay out your clothes where you will see them first thing in the morning. Reducing the number of decisions you have to make helps you stay on track when your motivation feels low. 

Keep your kit simple and functional. When the barrier to entry is low, you are much more likely to follow through with your movement goals.

## Make it practical

Start by choosing one small habit, such as walking to the station or doing ten minutes of movement after dinner. Do not worry about being perfect every single day. Focus on keeping the rhythm of your routine alive so that movement becomes a natural part of your Tokyo lifestyle.
