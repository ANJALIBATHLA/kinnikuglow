You are likely feeling the weight of a long commute and a demanding schedule in Tokyo while trying to keep your health on track. Balancing everything is rarely about perfection and almost always about how you structure your limited free time.

## Realistic meal prep

Cooking every night after a late shift at the office is rarely sustainable. I find it most helpful to focus on simple components rather than full recipes. 

[IMAGE: a collection of glass containers filled with pre-washed greens and cooked grains]

Buy pre-cut vegetables at your local supermarket like Life or Summit to save time on busy weeknights. Keep your freezer stocked with frozen salmon fillets or pre-marinated chicken breast. You can quickly steam these with a bag of frozen vegetables in a frying pan when you get home. Focus on keeping your fridge stocked with basic protein and fiber so that you always have a ten minute dinner ready.

## Fitting in movement

Do not aim for a one hour gym session if your schedule only allows for twenty minutes. Consistency matters more than the duration of your workout.

If you have a gym membership, choose a location that is either on your walk home from the train station or inside your office building. Convenience removes the mental barrier of needing to go out of your way. On days when you cannot make it to the gym, use the stairs at your station or walk an extra stop before hopping on the train. Movement is cumulative. It does not have to happen all at once.

## Simplifying your schedule

You will burn out quickly if you try to optimize every hour of the day. Prioritize what matters most to your energy levels for the week ahead.

Write down your top three tasks for work and your two main health goals every Sunday. When your professional workload increases, allow your fitness routine to become more flexible. It is perfectly fine to swap a heavy weightlifting session for a quick walk through a park like Shinjuku Gyoen or Yoyogi Park on days when your brain is tired. 

## Staying consistent

Choose one day each week to reset your kitchen and your calendar. Clear out the old produce from your fridge and plan for the next five days of lunches. Taking this small step prevents the panic of having nothing to eat during your lunch break. You do not need a perfect system. You just need a routine that you can actually maintain when your job gets busy.
