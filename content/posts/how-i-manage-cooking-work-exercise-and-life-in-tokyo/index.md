You are likely staring at your train schedule wondering how you can possibly fit a gym session and a home cooked dinner into your evening. Balancing a full time job in Tokyo with personal health goals often feels like a constant game of catch up.

## Simplify your meal prep

Trying to cook elaborate meals every night after a long day at the office is a quick way to burn out. Instead, focus on batch cooking base ingredients on Sunday. I keep a large container of steamed broccoli, boiled eggs, and grilled chicken breast in my fridge at all times. 

These items serve as the foundation for quick meals throughout the week. If I am too tired to cook, I pick up a package of pre washed salad greens or frozen vegetables from a local supermarket like Life or Aeon. This approach stops the temptation to rely on convenience store bento boxes every night.

## Build fitness into your commute

You do not need an hour of dedicated gym time to stay active in this city. Walking is the most effective way to incorporate movement into a busy Tokyo work life. I often get off one station early to add an extra fifteen minutes of walking to my morning or evening commute.

If you have a gym membership, choose a location that is either on your direct train line or within walking distance of your office. Removing the friction of traveling to a different neighborhood makes it much easier to go after work. When I am short on time, I opt for short high intensity workouts at home instead of commuting to a facility.

## Establish small daily routines

Consistency matters more than intensity when your schedule is packed. I find that keeping my gym bag packed and placed by the door saves me from skipping sessions when I feel tired. If the bag is already ready, I have one less decision to make before I leave the house.

Designate specific days for high energy tasks like intense workouts or grocery shopping. Use the quieter evenings for lighter activities like stretching or meal planning. Respecting your natural energy levels helps you maintain your habits for months instead of just a few days.

## Focus on your own pace

It is easy to compare your routine to what you see on social media, but your reality is unique to your job and your commute. Aim for progress rather than perfection. If you only manage a ten minute workout or a very simple dinner, that is still a win. 

Start by picking one habit to focus on this week, such as walking for ten extra minutes or preparing one source of protein for your fridge. Build from there as you get comfortable with your routine.
